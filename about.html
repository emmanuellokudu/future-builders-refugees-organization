About FBRO

A refugee-led community-based organization founded in 2024 in Kakuma Refugee Camp, Kenya.

Why FBRO exists

Many families in Kakuma Refugee Camp continue to face social and economic challenges that affect their daily lives. Young people often lack opportunities for skills training and income generation, while vulnerable families struggle to meet basic needs.

FBRO was started by refugees who wanted to respond to these everyday challenges: unemployment, lack of opportunities for youth, poverty, conflict, environmental problems, and limited access to education and skills development.

It was formed by community members who believed that local communities can create positive change when people work together and support one another. Since its founding, FBRO has mobilized volunteers and community members to take part in activities that promote peacebuilding, youth empowerment, environmental protection, education, and livelihood support.

Our vision

A peaceful, empowered, and self-reliant community where refugees and host communities live with dignity and hope.

Our mission

To support refugees and vulnerable host communities through community-led programs that promote livelihoods, education, peacebuilding, environmental conservation, and youth empowerment.

Our objectives

  • To empower youth, women, and vulnerable families through skills development and livelihood opportunities.
  • To promote peace and peaceful coexistence among refugee and host communities.
  • To support education, mentorship, and learning opportunities for children and youth.
  • To improve food security and household income through farming, poultry keeping, and other livelihood activities.
  • To promote environmental conservation through tree planting, environmental awareness, and sustainable community practices.

How we work

FBRO believes communities understand their challenges best. We involve community members in identifying problems, planning activities, and implementing local solutions.

We work closely with youth groups, women groups, community leaders, refugee-led organizations, and other stakeholders to create positive and sustainable community impact.

Who we work with

  • Refugees living in Kakuma Refugee Camp
  • Vulnerable host community members
  • Youth and adolescents
  • Women and girls
  • Persons with disabilities
  • Elderly people
  • Low-income households
